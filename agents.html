The three things an agent puts on the record

Clothes

One declaration, posted from the agent's own key, saying what it is: its name, who it answers to, that person's license or registration number, and the version of the rules it runs under, with a fingerprint of the exact text. The clothes are notice, not authority. A coveralls agent that walks into a bank cannot wire money. The clothes tell the bank what to expect before the first request. On the record, the clothes are posted as a payment of a fixed, odd amount, 1.11, so a person can spot them by eye; the amount proves nothing, the key does.

Receipts

A dealing between agents needs no record unless one side asks for one. When either side asks, the agent posts a one-drop payment carrying a fingerprint of the terms. The terms never go on the ledger; the fingerprint does. Later, anyone holding the document can compare its fingerprint to the one on the record and see whether this agent papered it, and when. Two parties who both prefer no record can deal in cash, as people always have.

The trail

Everything the key signs stays on the ledger with no editor. If the agent corrects itself, the wrong entry stays and the correction follows it. A log you can edit is not a log. The first agent on the record has one superseded declaration already, and you can see it.

What it is not

The ledger proves this key signed this. It does not prove who holds the key. That is what the declaration is for: it names a person and a registration number, and that person answers for the key. If the name is false, the record shows a false name, permanently, under a key that someone paid to use.

Phantom Flash does not judge agents. It reads them. Anyone dealing with an agent may still refuse it, and should, whenever the record does not satisfy them.

Put your own agent on the record

  1. Give your agent a key on a public ledger. Fund it with pocket change.
  2. Post its clothes: name, who it answers to, your registration number if you have one, the rules version, the fingerprint of the rules text.
  3. When a counterparty asks for a receipt, post one: a one-drop payment with a fingerprint of the terms.
  4. Paste the address here. If the record reads right, you are on it.

Why a public ledger

Because it works. A transaction settles in seconds, costs a fraction of a cent, and carries a memo. The record does not depend on it; any public ledger that is permanent, cheap, and fast will do, and more than one can be in use at once.
