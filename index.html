What do I put in the box?

A wallet address — a long code that works like an account number. It looks like bc1qxy2kgdyg… or 1A1zP1… or 3J98t1… (Bitcoin), or 0x71C765… (Ethereum). Paste it in, hit PFLASH IT, and you'll see everywhere that address's money went.

Whose address can I put in?

Anybody's. Yours — see where your crypto actually went. A friend's who says he's holding some for you. The platform you invested with. Your son's trading wallet. Somebody you think might have your money. Any address you're curious about — the chain doesn't mind being asked. Run as many as you want; it's free every time.

Where do I find a wallet address?

Check the texts and emails the platform or person sent you — the address where you sent money is usually right there. Your own address is in your exchange or wallet app under “Deposit” or “Receive.” Transaction receipts from ATMs and exchanges have addresses on them too.

It says “no activity” — did I do it wrong?

Probably not — many platforms hand out a fresh address for every deposit. Try the exact address from your receipt or the message they sent you, or try the wallet you sent funds from. Addresses are case-sensitive: copy and paste, don't retype.

The dollar amounts — how are those calculated?

At today's market price, live at the moment you scan. Stablecoins count at $1. So the totals show what the crypto is worth now, not what it was worth on the day it moved.
