The Story story.html

In October, R.M. received a text meant for "someone else." The sender, calling herself Mira, apologized, made small talk, and kept talking. Within three weeks she had introduced R.M. to the trading platform where she claimed to have made her money: NovaTradeFX — a polished site with live charts, an account dashboard, and a 24/7 "account manager."

Over nine weeks, R.M. made 14 deposits totaling $187,400 — first from a Coinbase account, later through two Bitcoin ATMs when "faster funding" was suggested. The dashboard showed steady wins. By January the balance read $213,000.

Then R.M. tried to withdraw. The platform demanded a 15% "profit tax" — payable in advance, in Bitcoin. Then a "compliance verification fee." Then the account manager stopped answering.

What the chain showed: the balance was a picture. The deposits never sat in a trading account — they left the platform's deposit wallet within hours of arrival, every time, along the same paths, to the same destination. The $213,000 never existed. The $187,400 was real — and it went somewhere. Finding where is the rest of this report.
